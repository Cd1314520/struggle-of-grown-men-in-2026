# Account, terms-of-service and legal risk of automated job searching on LinkedIn and Indeed (US individual, as of 2026-10-06)

Scope note: individual job seeker, US, home residential IP, read-only job-search queries a few dozen to a few hundred times a day. Not legal advice. Terms wording below was pulled directly from the live pages on 2026-10-06 (raw HTML fetched and text-extracted, so quotes are verbatim). Evidence-strength labels used throughout: **[DOC]** = primary document (terms, help page, court record, official report); **[PRESS]** = reputable news / law-firm commentary; **[VENDOR]** = blog of a company that sells automation/scraping tools (interested party, weak); **[ANEC]** = individual user report (weak, uncontrolled).

## 1. LinkedIn: exact terms wording, and what enforcement looks like in 2025-2026

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's terms flatly prohibit any scraping or bot access, with no carve-out for read-only or personal use, and they purport to bind logged-out "Visitors" as well as Members; the stated sanction is account restriction, suspension or termination. In practice enforcement is real but graduated (temporary restriction with auto re-enable, then ID verification through Persona, then permanent restriction), and almost all the documented triggers are high-volume logged-in actions (auto-apply, connection/message automation, profile scraping); I found no documented case of an account restricted for low-volume read-only job-search automation, and equally no evidence that such use is safe.

### Cited Findings

**User Agreement (page states "Effective on November 3, 2025")** — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) **[DOC]**
- Who is bound: "By creating a LinkedIn account or accessing or using our Services (described below), you are agreeing to enter into a legally binding contract with LinkedIn". Section 1.2: "This Contract applies to Members and Visitors. When you register and join the LinkedIn Services, you become a "Member". If you have chosen not to register for our Services, you may access certain features as a "Visitor."" — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Section 3.4 Limits: "LinkedIn reserves the right to limit your use of the Services, including the number of your connections and your ability to contact other Members. LinkedIn reserves the right to restrict, suspend, or terminate your account if you breach this Contract or the law or are misusing the Services (e.g., violating any of the Dos and Don'ts or Professional Community Policies)." — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Section 8.2 Don'ts, "You agree that you will not:" (relevant items, verbatim):
  - "Create a false identity on LinkedIn, misrepresent your identity, create a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person), or use or attempt to use another's account (such as sharing log-in credentials or copying cookies);"
  - "Develop, support or use software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons or any other technology) to scrape or copy the Services, including profiles and other data from the Services;"
  - "Override any security feature or bypass or circumvent any access controls or use limits of the Services (such as search results, profiles, or videos);"
  - "Copy, use, display or distribute any information (including content) obtained from the Services, whether directly or through third parties (such as search tools or data aggregators or brokers), without the consent of the content owner (such as LinkedIn for content it owns);"
  - "Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, add or download contacts, send or redirect messages, create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement;"
  - "Interfere with the operation of, or place an unreasonable load on, the Services (e.g., spam, denial of service attack, viruses, manipulating algorithms);"
  — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Section 5 Termination: "Both you and LinkedIn may terminate this Contract at any time with notice to the other. On termination, you lose the right to access or use the Services." — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- Forum/governing law for US users: California law; claims "can be litigated only in the federal or state courts in Santa Clara County, California, USA" (no arbitration clause for LinkedIn; as summarised from the page by a fetch tool, not re-read line by line). — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)
- The "copying cookies" language in the false-identity item is notable for tools that export an `li_at` session cookie into a separate scraper: the clause as written concerns using "another's account", so reusing one's own cookie is not literally covered by that item, but is covered by the scrape/bot items. — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)

**Help page "Prohibited software and extensions" (page shows "Last updated: 2 years ago")** — [LinkedIn Help a1341387](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) **[DOC]**
- "LinkedIn is committed to keeping its members' data safe and its website free from fraud and abuse. In order to protect our members' data and our website, we don't permit the use of any third party software, including "crawlers", bots, browser plug-ins, or browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website. We also don't permit the use of fake accounts or fake engagement on LinkedIn's website, including any tools or services that try to manipulate LinkedIn's content algorithms. All of these tools violate the User Agreement, including, but not limited to, the following prohibitions listed in Section 8.2:" (followed by the 8.2 items quoted above).
- Consequence wording: "Any member who uses tools for such purposes is in violation of the User Agreement. This means that they risk having their accounts restricted or shut down. They also risk the possibility that any prohibited tools they're using may become non-operational without notice. In order to maintain LinkedIn as a safe and trusted platform to facilitate authentic interactions between professionals and to keep our members' data safe, we're constantly working to improve our technical measures and defenses against the operation of scraping, automation, and other tools that abuse LinkedIn's platform."

**Help page "Automated activity on LinkedIn" (page shows "Last updated: 4 days ago" on 2026-10-06, i.e. about 2026-10-02)** — [LinkedIn Help a1340567](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567) **[DOC]**
- "In order to protect our members' privacy and keep LinkedIn a trusted platform for authentic interactions, we don't allow the use of third-party software or browser extensions that scrape, modify the appearance of, or automate activity on LinkedIn's website."
- "Third-party software that automates tasks could potentially export or scrape data from LinkedIn and affects authenticity; at times, without your consent. This is a violation of LinkedIn's User Agreement, and may be a violation of privacy legislation in specific jurisdictions."
- What LinkedIn itself says happens on a first automation flag: "If your account is restricted for automated activity, we ask that you take the following actions: Review and disable the software or extension that automates activities. Your account will then automatically be re-enabled at the time specified on the suspension notification." It also offers an appeal path ("please log in and contact us"), noted as "being gradually rolled out and is currently available only to some LinkedIn members."

**robots.txt** — [linkedin.com/robots.txt](https://www.linkedin.com/robots.txt) **[DOC]**
- Header: "Notice: The use of robots or other automated means to access LinkedIn without the express permission of LinkedIn is strictly prohibited." The catch-all rule is `User-agent: *` / `Disallow: /`.

**Identity verification (Persona)** — [LinkedIn Help a1342692, "Verify your identity to recover account access" (page shows "Last updated: 4 weeks ago")](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1342692) **[DOC]**
- Identity verification through Persona is the recovery route "If you can't recover or access your account or if your account has been restricted"; accepted documents are a digital ID or "a clear photo of your valid government issued ID card, driver's license, or passport"; the name on the ID must match the profile name; LinkedIn says submitted data is "generally permanently deleted within 14 days of submission". (Quotes via fetch-tool extraction of the page.)

**Scale of enforcement (official numbers concern fake accounts, not automation flags)** — [LinkedIn Community Report](https://about.linkedin.com/transparency/community-report) **[DOC]**
- For July-December 2025, LinkedIn says automated defenses blocked "97.8% of the fake accounts we stopped" and "99.7% of the fake accounts were stopped proactively, before a member report"; it says it has "improved models that are restricting more fake accounts at registration before they have a chance to share content."
- Older data point (2022): 16.4 million fake accounts stopped automatically at registration in Jan-Jun 2022, per search-result summary of earlier reports. — [LinkedIn Community Report](https://about.linkedin.com/transparency/community-report)

**Evidence on what triggers restrictions**
- AIHawk (open-source LinkedIn Easy Apply bot): Business Insider (Nov 6, 2024 — older) reported that LinkedIn restricted the account of the tool's creator, Federico Elia, in September 2024; LinkedIn "did not specifically answer" why. LinkedIn's spokesperson said the platform "does not allow third-party software that scrapes or automates activity on LinkedIn". Users interviewed had applied to roughly 500-1,000 jobs (one about 250 applications per three to six hours) and described account consequences as a risk they accepted; the article does not report that those ordinary users were banned. — [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/free-ai-tool-help-apply-110301118.html) **[PRESS]**
- A vendor blog claims LinkedIn "has been aggressively cracking down on automation since late 2025", restricting Easy Apply access and in some cases permanently banning users, and offers thresholds (under 15 Easy Apply/day "generally safe", 30+/day "red zone"). These numbers are unsourced marketing content from an auto-apply vendor. — [aiapplyd.com](https://aiapplyd.com/blog/auto-apply-linkedin-without-getting-banned) **[VENDOR]**
- The same vendor source says LazyApply has a 2.3/5 Trustpilot rating "with users consistently reporting LinkedIn restrictions". Not independently verified. — [aiapplyd.com](https://aiapplyd.com/blog/auto-apply-linkedin-without-getting-banned) **[VENDOR]**
- Vendor blogs describe the typical ladder as: automated flag that "often lifts in a few hours on its own", then Persona ID verification taking "1-3 days", then permanent restriction for repeat behaviour. — [postory.io](https://postory.io/blog/linkedin-account-restricted) **[VENDOR]**
- Logged-in profile scraping with an open-source crawler: a GitHub user reported "after I got several result, the account was banned from linkedin" (Oct 16, 2019 — old, single report, profile scraping not job search). — [scrapedin-linkedin-crawler issue #17](https://github.com/linkedtales/scrapedin-linkedin-crawler/issues/17) **[ANEC]**
- Cookie-based tools: a scraping-vendor review states that tools taking your LinkedIn cookie "make requests as you", and LinkedIn "flags the session within hours to days, restricts the account, and may require a CAPTCHA or phone verification to restore access". — [scrapfly.io](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/best-linkedin-scrapers-github) **[VENDOR]**
- LinkedIn's own litigation filings say it "routinely identifies" a commercial scraper's fake-account scraping "within hours of initiation" (ProAPIs complaint, as reported). This is about industrial-scale activity. — [The Record, Oct 3, 2025](https://therecord.media/linkedin-sues-data-scraping-company) **[PRESS]**

### Inferences
- The terms draw no distinction between read-only job search and write actions, between personal and commercial use, or between low and high volume. Any logged-in automation, including driving a real Chrome browser with a script, is a clear breach of 8.2 as written; the realistic question is detection, not permission.
- Every concrete restriction report I could find involves either (a) write actions at scale (Easy Apply, connection requests, messages) or (b) profile scraping. Job-search result pages are a lower-sensitivity surface (job posts are not member personal data, which is what LinkedIn's public statements and lawsuits emphasise), so read-only job-search automation is plausibly lower risk — but this is an inference from absence of reports, not positive evidence. Low-volume users who are never flagged do not file bug reports.
- LinkedIn's own help page indicates a first automation flag normally produces a time-limited restriction that auto-lifts once the tool is disabled, not immediate permanent loss. For a user whose profile matters, the realistic bad outcomes are (1) a temporary lockout during an active job search, and (2) being forced to hand a government ID to Persona to get back in; permanent loss appears to be reserved for repeat or severe cases, but LinkedIn has full contractual discretion.
- "A few hundred" logged-in search-page loads per day from one account is well above ordinary human job-search behaviour and would be the main detection risk; "a few dozen" is within human range.

### Gaps
- No official LinkedIn statistics on how many accounts are restricted for automation (the Community Report covers fake accounts, spam and scams, not automation flags).
- I could not retrieve 2025-2026 Reddit threads or AIHawk/linkedin-mcp-server GitHub issues directly (search results surfaced vendor blogs instead); the frequency of restrictions among auto-apply users is therefore unquantified. Vendor claims of a "late 2025 crackdown" are uncorroborated by primary sources.
- No source found that specifically documents an account restricted (or confirmed not restricted) for read-only, logged-in job-search scraping at low volume.
- LinkedIn's "commercial use limit" help page (monthly search cap) was not fetched; it concerns people/profile search and I did not confirm whether or how it affects job search.
- How often Persona ID prompts are triggered by automation flags vs. other causes is unknown; LinkedIn publishes no data.

## 2. LinkedIn dedicated / secondary accounts

### Takeaway
A dedicated second account is itself a direct breach of the User Agreement ("you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name"), and a pseudonymous one additionally breaches the false-identity clause — the same conduct (fake accounts) that features in every LinkedIn scraping lawsuit. It therefore converts a single violation (automation) into two or three, and unverified-but-consistent reports say LinkedIn links accounts by device, IP and phone and may restrict both.

### Cited Findings
- Section 2.1: "To use the Services, you agree that: (1) you must be the "Minimum Age"(described below) or older; (2) you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name; and (3) you are not already restricted by LinkedIn from using the Services. Creating an account with false information is a violation of our terms, including accounts registered on behalf of others or persons under the age of 16." — [LinkedIn User Agreement, effective Nov 3, 2025](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) **[DOC]**
- Section 2.2: "You agree to: (1) protect against wrongful access to your account (e.g., use a strong password and keep it confidential); (2) not share or transfer your account or any part of it ... and (3) follow the law, our list of Dos and Don'ts (below), and our Professional Community Policies. ... You are responsible for anything that happens through your account unless you close it or report misuse." — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) **[DOC]**
- Section 8.1 Dos: "Provide accurate contact and identity information to us and keep it updated; Use your real name on your profile". Section 8.2: "Create a false identity on LinkedIn, misrepresent your identity, create a Member profile for anyone other than yourself (a real person)". — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) **[DOC]**
- Note clause (3) of 2.1: a person "already restricted by LinkedIn" may not use the Services, so opening a new account after a restriction is separately prohibited. — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) **[DOC]**
- Help page: "We also don't permit the use of fake accounts or fake engagement on LinkedIn's website". — [LinkedIn Help a1341387](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387) **[DOC]**
- Registration-time defenses: LinkedIn reports that most fake accounts it stops are blocked by automated defenses (97.8% in Jul-Dec 2025) and that models are "restricting more fake accounts at registration". — [LinkedIn Community Report](https://about.linkedin.com/transparency/community-report) **[DOC]**
- Fake accounts are the fact pattern LinkedIn litigates: hiQ's consent judgment included CFAA liability for "direct access to password-protected pages on LinkedIn's platforms using fake accounts"; Mantheos (2022) used "hundreds of fake LinkedIn member accounts"; Proxycurl (2025) allegedly "hundreds of thousands of fake accounts"; ProAPIs (2025) allegedly "millions". — [National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/linkedin-s-data-scraping-battle-hiq-labs-ends-proposed-judgment) **[PRESS]**; [lobstr.io](https://www.lobstr.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal) **[VENDOR]**; [The Record](https://therecord.media/linkedin-sues-data-scraping-company) **[PRESS]**
- Spillover claims: vendor blogs state that "Duplicate accounts violate the User Agreement and get both profiles restricted", that LinkedIn "detects duplicate profiles via IP matching and usage patterns and acts without prior warning", and flags "same device/browser fingerprint, same IP address (especially if residential/static), reuse of phone numbers or emails". One lists "opening a second account" as one of the moves that turns a temporary restriction into a permanent one. None cite LinkedIn documentation or data. — [evaboot.com](https://evaboot.com/blog/how-to-manage-multiple-linkedin-accounts), [postory.io](https://postory.io/blog/linkedin-account-restricted), [leadcrm.io](https://www.leadcrm.io/blog/can-i-have-two-linkedin-accounts/) **[VENDOR]**
- Restricted accounts (including new ones) are routed to Persona government-ID verification, and the ID name must match the profile name — which a pseudonymous account cannot satisfy, and a real-name duplicate would expose as a duplicate. — [LinkedIn Help a1342692](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1342692) **[DOC]**

### Inferences
- A second account does not reduce contractual exposure; it increases it. It is defensible only as a practical "blast-radius" measure, and that only works if LinkedIn does not link the two accounts — which from a single home IP and the same machine/browser is exactly the condition under which linkage is most plausible.
- A fresh, connection-less, low-activity account that immediately shows automated behaviour matches the profile of the fake accounts LinkedIn's models are tuned to restrict at or shortly after registration, so a dedicated account is likely to be less durable than an aged real account, and may be dead-ended at a Persona ID check that it cannot pass without revealing the duplicate.
- For this user (real profile matters; single residential IP), the ranking implied by the terms and the evidence is: logged-out (no account at risk) > real account used at human-like volume in a real browser (one violation, graduated enforcement) > dedicated secondary account (two to three violations, lawsuit-pattern conduct, possible spillover). The middle and last options' relative ordering is judgment, not established fact.

### Gaps
- No primary-source evidence (LinkedIn statement, court filing, or controlled test) on whether LinkedIn restricts a main account because a second account on the same IP/device/phone was banned. All spillover claims are vendor assertions; strength: weak-to-moderate (consistent across vendors, plausible, unverified).
- No reliable 2026 data on current signup friction (whether phone verification is always required, how often new accounts get an immediate ID/Persona prompt). I found no primary source; anecdotes were not retrievable.
- No data on time-to-restriction for new automated low-activity accounts.

## 3. LinkedIn logged-out (guest) scraping

### Takeaway
With no account involved, the only consequences documented for an individual are technical: HTTP 429 rate limiting and IP-level blocking (reported after roughly ten result pages from one IP in JobSpy's documentation) and auth-wall redirects. I found no evidence either way on whether LinkedIn associates logged-out traffic from the same IP/browser with a logged-in account and penalises that account.

### Cited Findings
- JobSpy (widely used open-source scraper using LinkedIn's public guest job-search endpoints, no login) documents: "LinkedIn is the most restrictive and usually rate limits around the 10th page with one ip", and a 429 response "indicates that you have been blocked by the job board site for sending too many requests"; recommended mitigations are waiting between scrapes and changing IP. — [python-jobspy on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/python-jobspy/1.1.54) **[ANEC/maintainer documentation — moderately strong, reflects many users' experience]**
- LinkedIn's public guest endpoints allow job search with keyword/location/remote/experience/date filters "with no login and no cookies required" (third-party JS port documentation). — [jobspy-js docs](https://www.mintlify.com/borgius/jobspy-js/job-boards/linkedin) **[ANEC]**
- A 2026 scraping-vendor test reports JobSpy returning real LinkedIn and Indeed listings in a test run, while other boards failed; describes results as varying by day. — [scrapfly.io](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/best-open-source-job-scrapers) **[VENDOR]**
- A scraping vendor notes that logged-out scrapers mean "there is no account for LinkedIn to restrict". — [scrapfly.io](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/best-linkedin-scrapers-github) **[VENDOR]**
- Contractually, LinkedIn asserts its agreement binds logged-out "Visitors" ("This Contract applies to Members and Visitors") and robots.txt disallows all unlisted bots. — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), [robots.txt](https://www.linkedin.com/robots.txt) **[DOC]**
- Courts have treated logged-out scraping of public data as outside the CFAA (hiQ, 9th Cir. 2022) and, for Meta's differently worded terms, outside the contract (Meta v. Bright Data, 2024) — see section 5. — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators), [Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm) **[PRESS]**

### Inferences
- At a few dozen queries a day spread out with delays, guest scraping from a home IP should mostly stay under the reported ~10-page burst threshold; at a few hundred a day, intermittent 429s are likely and need back-off rather than proxy rotation (rotating proxies moves the conduct closer to "bypass or circumvent any access controls").
- Because the home IP is shared with the user's normal logged-in LinkedIn use, an IP-level block or challenge would degrade the user's ordinary browsing too (e.g. CAPTCHA/verification prompts on login). That is a nuisance risk, distinct from an account sanction. This is an inference; no source documents it.
- Running the logged-out scraper in a separate browser profile/HTTP client with no LinkedIn cookies is the obvious way to avoid handing LinkedIn a direct link between the scraper and the real account; whether IP alone is used to link them is unknown.

### Gaps
- No source (official or anecdotal) found on whether LinkedIn links logged-out traffic to a logged-in account on the same IP/browser and restricts the account. Absence of reports is weakly reassuring only.
- Duration of LinkedIn guest-endpoint IP blocks (minutes vs. days) is not documented in any source I retrieved.
- LinkedIn's Visitor-binding language has not, to my knowledge from sources retrieved here, been tested in court against a logged-out scraper post-Meta v. Bright Data.

## 4. Indeed: terms, enforcement, whether an account is needed

### Takeaway
Indeed's Terms (updated one week ago, September 29, 2026) prohibit any bot/scraper/"AI or Agentic AI" access without written permission, bind anyone "accessing or using" the site, and let Indeed disable accounts for any reason — but job search needs no account, and the enforcement actually reported is technical (Cloudflare Turnstile / 403), not account-based. Indeed's terms also contain a US arbitration clause and newly reference "Permitted AI Connector" integrations as the sanctioned route for AI access.

### Cited Findings
All from [Indeed Terms of Service, "Last Updated: September 29, 2026"](https://www.indeed.com/legal) **[DOC]** unless noted.
- Who is bound: "By accessing or using any part of our Site, including via an API (defined below), you agree to be bound by the Terms then in effect with the applicable contracting entity set forth below. If you do not agree, you may not access or use our Site."
- Section 21 Site Rules preamble: "Your use of the Site is conditioned on your adherence to these rules and any other policies or rules listed on the Site. Failure to comply is a material breach of the Terms. Indeed may, at its discretion and without notice, take measures against users or activity violating rules, including content removal, service restrictions, logging out, or account disabling."
- Site Rules, "DO NOT, directly or indirectly:" (relevant items, verbatim):
  - "Access the Site through any means other than the public interfaces provided by Indeed;"
  - "Access any data, especially personal data, by automated means or otherwise, without permission;"
  - "Use any automated system (bots, scrapers, spiders, AI or Agentic AI) to access, data-mine, or submit content to the Site, in bulk or otherwise, without Indeed's express written permission (we conditionally grant permission to crawl the Site solely as outlined in our robots.txt file). You may not crawl, scrape, extract data from, reproduce, duplicate, copy, sell, exploit, trade or resell any part of the Site or access the Site for the development, training, fine-tuning, or improvement of any third-party machine learning model, artificial intelligence (AI) system, or any related software program, model, algorithm, or generative AI tool;"
  - "Submit job applications or other User Content by automated means, in bulk or otherwise, other than by automated tools that the Site explicitly offers or that have been otherwise agreed to in writing with Indeed;"
  - "Use or misappropriate the Site for your own commercial gain;"
- Site Rules closing: "Indeed reserves the right to use technical measures to detect, investigate, or prevent unauthorized automated access or activity on the Site."
- Job-seeker section, Limits: "Further, you acknowledge that Indeed may require that users verify their email address or phone number to use the site or apply to jobs. Use of any automation, scripting, or bots to automate the Indeed Apply process outside of Indeed's official vendors and tooling is prohibited."
- Accounts: "Account Creation: Some areas of the Site require registration or account creation. You are responsible for safeguarding your account credentials. You agree to provide true, accurate, current, and complete information. You may not create multiple accounts or use automated means to create accounts."
- Account security: "If you intentionally or negligently allow any other person or automated system to access or use your account, you are, to the extent permitted by applicable law, fully responsible for all actions taken through your account."
- Termination: "Indeed may, at its sole discretion, suspend or terminate your account, remove your content, or restrict, suspend or terminate your access to any part of the Site, at any time, for any or no reason, without prior notice or liability." Also, near the top: "Indeed may disable any account or restrict your ability to use all or parts of the Site, for any or no reason without notice."
- Third-party AI: "Third Party Automations: You agree that all general Site access and use prohibitions in the Terms, including those against scraping, bots, and other automated activity, remain in full effect. However, Indeed may, at our sole discretion, offer you the ability to connect your Indeed Account to third-party AI platforms ("Permitted AI Connector")." It is described as Beta.
- Arbitration: Section D.12 "CONTAINS AN ARBITRATION AGREEMENT THAT APPLIES TO ALL USERS LOCATED IN THE UNITED STATES"; "AS A JOB SEEKER, YOU MAY OPT OUT OF THE ARBITRATION AGREEMENT PURSUANT TO SECTION D.12(k)".
- Monitoring: "Indeed collects, records, processes, analyzes, and stores (including via automated means) all your activities, interactions, and communications on or through the Site. This includes visited URLs, viewed content, input info, clicks, scrolls, hovers, and time spent ... from all users, including those in private browsing modes."
- robots.txt: the default `User-agent: *` block begins `Allow: /` and explicitly allows result pagination `&start=0` through `&start=90`, while disallowing many paths (e.g. `/applystart`, `/advanced_search`, `/alert`, `/api/getrecjobs`, `/company/*`). The Terms tie the only standing crawl permission to this file. — [indeed.com/robots.txt](https://www.indeed.com/robots.txt) **[DOC]** (I read only the first ~60 lines; the full file should be checked for the exact `/jobs` and `/viewjob` rules before relying on it.)
- Whether an account is needed: the Terms say only that "Some areas of the Site require registration"; job search is publicly reachable, and scrapers such as JobSpy query Indeed without login. — [Indeed Terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal) **[DOC]**; [python-jobspy](https://pypi.org/project/python-jobspy/1.1.54) **[ANEC]**
- Enforcement in practice, conflicting reports:
  - JobSpy documentation: "Indeed is the best scraper currently with no rate limiting". — [python-jobspy on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/python-jobspy/1.1.54) **[ANEC/maintainer]** (statement is from older package docs; date uncertain)
  - A 2026 scraping-vendor guide says Indeed uses Cloudflare; direct requests to job-listing URLs return 403 with a Cloudflare Turnstile CAPTCHA page; a real-browser session that first visits the homepage obtains cookies that let subsequent pages load. — [scrapfly.io, "How to Scrape Indeed.com (2026 Update)"](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-indeedcom) **[VENDOR]**
  - A scraping-library developer described Indeed job search as using "custom" Cloudflare CAPTCHA variants (May 2025). — [Scrapling on X](https://x.com/Scrapling_dev/status/1920483167456154022) **[ANEC]**
  - The two are reconcilable: JobSpy uses Indeed's mobile-app API endpoint rather than the Cloudflare-fronted website (not verified in a source retrieved here — treat as unconfirmed).

### Inferences
- An Indeed account adds no value for read-only search and creates the only thing Indeed could take away; logged-out is the obvious choice for Indeed. If the user has a real Indeed account with application history, keep the scraper in a cookie-less client so it is not attached to it.
- The realistic Indeed consequence for logged-out low-volume scraping is CAPTCHA/403 friction on the home IP (which would also affect the user's manual browsing), not any account or legal action.
- Indeed's express mention of "AI or Agentic AI" and of browser-automation-style access ("any means other than the public interfaces") means driving a real Chrome session with a script or an AI agent is squarely covered if logged in; the terms claim to cover it logged-out too, but enforceability against a non-account-holder is weaker (see section 5).
- The robots.txt permission is narrow but real: polite crawling of allowed search-result paths is the one form of automated access the Terms "conditionally" permit. Whether a personal scraper qualifies as a crawler within that permission is untested; it is nonetheless the most defensible posture (respect robots.txt, low rate, identify honestly).

### Gaps
- I found no reports (GitHub, press, or vendor) of Indeed suspending a job-seeker account for search-only automation, and none retrievable for auto-apply either. Absence of evidence; Reddit was not directly searchable.
- No official Indeed statement or statistic on enforcement against scrapers.

## 5. Legal context (brief; not legal advice)

### Takeaway
For public data accessed without logging in, US federal anti-hacking law (CFAA) has been held not to apply (hiQ, following Van Buren), and platform contract claims have failed where the scraper was logged out (Meta v. Bright Data) or no damage was shown (X Corp. v. Bright Data). Once a user logs in, they have unambiguously accepted the terms and breach-of-contract exposure is real in principle (hiQ on remand), but every LinkedIn lawsuit found targets commercial resellers using fake accounts at industrial scale; none targets an individual doing personal-use job search.

### Cited Findings
- **Van Buren v. United States (US Supreme Court, June 3, 2021, 6-3)**: a person "exceeds authorized access" under the CFAA only when he "accesses a computer with authorization but then obtains information located in particular areas of the computer – such as files, folders or databases – that are off limits to him"; the Court rejected the theory that accessing permitted information for an improper purpose (contrary to a policy, contract or terms of service) violates the statute. — [Cooley alert](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2021/2021-06-09-us-supreme-court-computer-fraud-abuse-act-van-buren), [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Buren_v._United_States) **[PRESS]**
- **hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn (9th Cir., April 2022, on remand from the Supreme Court after Van Buren)**: affirmed that the CFAA's "without authorization" concept does not apply to public websites; "violating a public website's user agreement alone is insufficient to trigger CFAA liability" (law-firm paraphrase). — [Morgan Lewis, Dec 2022](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators) **[PRESS]**
- **hiQ on remand (N.D. Cal., November 2022)**: the district court held that user-agreement provisions prohibiting scraping and fake profiles are enforceable through a breach-of-contract claim; hiQ's waiver/estoppel defenses raised fact disputes. — [National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/linkedin-s-data-scraping-battle-hiq-labs-ends-proposed-judgment), [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators) **[PRESS]**
- **hiQ consent judgment (stipulated Dec 6-7, 2022)**: $500,000 judgment against hiQ; stipulated liability for breach of the User Agreement, CFAA violation "based on hiQ's direct access to password-protected pages on LinkedIn's platforms using fake accounts", California's computer-access statute, trespass to chattels and misappropriation; permanent injunction to stop scraping and destroy source code, data and algorithms derived from scraped profiles. — [National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/linkedin-s-data-scraping-battle-hiq-labs-ends-proposed-judgment), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/380493/web-scraper-agrees-to-pay-linkedin-500000-destr.html) **[PRESS]**
- **Meta Platforms v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge Edward Chen, Jan 23, 2024; 2024 WL 251406)**: summary judgment for Bright Data on breach of contract. The court found no evidence of logged-in scraping; held Facebook/Instagram terms govern "use" by account holders and did not reach logged-out scraping of public data; held that Bright Data's having accounts did not matter because logged-out scraping was "unrelated to the purpose of its accounts" and so not "using" Facebook as contemplated by the Terms; and refused to enforce a perpetual survival clause after account termination. The ruling addresses contract only. — [Eric Goldman's Technology & Marketing Law Blog (guest post)](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm), [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/post/102kqw7/) **[PRESS]**
- **X Corp. v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge William Alsup, May 2024)**: claims dismissed; "Under California law, '[a] breach of contract without damage is not actionable'" and "X Corp. has not alleged any damage resulting from access through unauthorized means"; the court also found X's scraping-and-selling claims in tension with federal copyright law (preemption) per coverage titles. — [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/395944/judge-throws-out-xs-claims-against-data-scraper.html), [CNBC, May 10, 2024](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/05/10/elon-musks-x-loses-lawsuit-against-bright-data-over-data-scraping.html) **[PRESS]**
- **LinkedIn v. Nubela / Proxycurl (N.D. Cal., filed Jan 24, 2025)**: alleged hundreds of thousands of fake accounts used to scrape millions of profiles including non-public data, resold via API; six claims including breach of contract, fraud, CFAA, California UCL, Lanham Act, misappropriation (per secondary coverage). LinkedIn announced on July 28, 2025 that it had "successfully resolved its lawsuit"; Proxycurl shut down in July 2025; secondary sources report a permanent injunction and data deletion. — [LinkedIn Newsroom, July 28, 2025](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/LinkedInWinsLegalBattleToProtectMemberData) **[DOC]**; [linkedapi.io](https://linkedapi.io/guides/proxycurl-alternatives), [lobstr.io](https://www.lobstr.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal) **[VENDOR]**
- **LinkedIn v. ProAPIs Inc. and CEO Rahmat Alam (N.D. Cal., case 5:25-cv-08393, filed early Oct 2025)**: alleged "industrial-scale fake account mill" creating "hundreds if not thousands" of fake accounts daily, scraping data "only available behind LinkedIn's password wall", charging customers up to $15,000/month; founder named personally. A vendor source reports the parties agreed to settle in February 2026 (not confirmed in a primary source). — [The Record, Oct 3, 2025](https://therecord.media/linkedin-sues-data-scraping-company) **[PRESS]**; [lobstr.io, updated Sept 17, 2026](https://www.lobstr.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal) **[VENDOR]**
- **LinkedIn v. Mantheos (2022 — older)**: Singapore firm used "hundreds of fake LinkedIn member accounts and virtual debit cards under fake names" to buy Sales Navigator seats and scrape behind login; sued February 2022, settled May 2022. — [lobstr.io](https://www.lobstr.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal) **[VENDOR]**
- Pattern: a 2026 review of LinkedIn's lawsuits found "no evidence of LinkedIn suing individual users" for personal scraping; every suit targets commercial operations running fake accounts and reselling data. (Vendor source, but consistent with every case found in this research.) — [lobstr.io](https://www.lobstr.io/blog/is-linkedin-scraping-legal) **[VENDOR]**
- Contract terms relevant to exposure: LinkedIn's agreement binds "Members and Visitors" by "accessing or using"; Indeed's binds anyone "accessing or using any part of our Site" and routes US disputes to arbitration with a job-seeker opt-out. — [LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), [Indeed Terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal) **[DOC]**

### Inferences
- Logged-out, public job listings, low volume, personal use: CFAA exposure is remote under Van Buren/hiQ (nothing is behind a gate). Contract exposure depends on whether a browsewrap-style "by accessing you agree" term binds a visitor who never clicked assent; Meta v. Bright Data turned on Meta's specific wording, and LinkedIn's and Indeed's terms are drafted more broadly (expressly covering visitors/any access), so that case does not automatically transfer. Even so, X Corp. v. Bright Data shows a contract claim needs actual damage, which a few hundred page loads a day cannot plausibly supply. Realistic consequence: IP blocks.
- Logged-in with a real account: the user has clearly accepted the terms, so automation is a breach of contract. The realistic remedy is the contractual one LinkedIn/Indeed reserve — restricting or terminating the account — not a lawsuit; damages from one individual's read-only job search would be negligible, and no such suit was found. CFAA risk rises only if one circumvents technical barriers after being blocked or uses fake accounts to get behind the login wall (the hiQ consent-judgment and Proxycurl/ProAPIs fact pattern).
- Dedicated fake/secondary account: this is the one choice that moves an individual's conduct toward the pleaded fact pattern in LinkedIn's suits (fake accounts + logged-in scraping, pleaded as breach, fraud and CFAA). Scale and resale are absent, so a suit remains very unlikely, but it is the legally worst-looking of the three options.
- Circumvention aggravates everything: rotating proxies, CAPTCHA-solving and fingerprint spoofing to defeat a block are the facts courts and plaintiffs point to ("bypass or circumvent any access controls"); backing off when blocked keeps the conduct in the benign category.
- Job postings are employer-authored commercial content rather than member personal data, so the privacy-law framing LinkedIn uses against profile scrapers has little purchase on job-search scraping for personal use.

### Gaps
- The 2019 Ninth Circuit hiQ opinion (preliminary-injunction affirmance) and the Supreme Court's 2021 GVR were not separately re-verified in a source fetched for these notes; the April 2022 re-affirmance is sourced. The district judge on the hiQ remand was not confirmed in a fetched source.
- Terms and court record of the Proxycurl resolution (injunction text) and the reported February 2026 ProAPIs settlement were not confirmed from a docket or primary document.
- I did not find Indeed-initiated scraping litigation of any kind, nor any 2025-2026 appellate ruling changing the hiQ/Van Buren framework; a targeted docket search was not done, so "none found" is not "none exist".
- State-law theories (California Penal Code 502, trespass to chattels) were not researched beyond their appearance in the hiQ consent judgment.

## 6. Reports of action against individual job seekers using auto-apply or job-scraping tools

### Takeaway
Documented action against individual job seekers is limited to account-level measures on LinkedIn (restriction, sometimes permanent), reported mainly for high-volume auto-apply tools; the best-documented single case is the AIHawk developer's own account being restricted in September 2024. I found no report of legal action against any individual job seeker by LinkedIn or Indeed, no report of Indeed account suspensions, and no report of account action tied to logged-out scrapers such as JobSpy.

### Cited Findings
- AIHawk / Auto_Jobs_Applier: creator Federico Elia's LinkedIn account was restricted in September 2024; LinkedIn would not say why, and would not say specifically whether using AIHawk violates the User Agreement beyond its general statement that it "does not allow third-party software that scrapes or automates activity on LinkedIn". Interviewed users (500-1,000 applications each) acknowledged risk ("he could face repercussions from LinkedIn") but none was reported as banned. (Nov 6, 2024 — older.) — [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/free-ai-tool-help-apply-110301118.html) **[PRESS; strength: one confirmed case, of a high-profile developer]**
- LazyApply: an auto-apply competitor's blog says users are "consistently reporting LinkedIn restrictions" and cites a 2.3/5 Trustpilot rating. — [aiapplyd.com](https://aiapplyd.com/blog/auto-apply-linkedin-without-getting-banned) **[VENDOR; strength: weak — competitor marketing, no linked reports]**
- General auto-apply: same source describes outcomes as losing Easy Apply access, inability to message, reduced profile visibility, recovery taking weeks, "some users never get full access back". — [aiapplyd.com](https://aiapplyd.com/blog/auto-apply-linkedin-without-getting-banned) **[VENDOR; weak]**
- Logged-in open-source scrapers: one 2019 GitHub report of an account "banned" after obtaining "several result[s]" with a logged-in profile crawler. — [scrapedin-linkedin-crawler issue #17](https://github.com/linkedtales/scrapedin-linkedin-crawler/issues/17) **[ANEC; single, old]**. A 2026 vendor review says users of cookie-based GitHub scrapers "report being permanently banned ... sometimes without warning". — [scrapfly.io](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/best-linkedin-scrapers-github) **[VENDOR; weak]**
- JobSpy (logged-out): the only consequences in its documentation are 429 rate limits/IP blocks; no account is used, so no account action is possible. — [python-jobspy on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/python-jobspy/1.1.54) **[ANEC/maintainer]**
- LinkedIn's own description of what happens to a member flagged for automation: time-limited restriction that auto-re-enables after the tool is disabled. — [LinkedIn Help a1340567](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567) **[DOC]**
- Indeed prohibits automating Indeed Apply and reserves "service restrictions, logging out, or account disabling", and may limit daily applications. — [Indeed Terms, Sept 29, 2026](https://www.indeed.com/legal) **[DOC]**

### Inferences
- Overall anecdotal evidence strength: moderate that high-volume LinkedIn auto-apply leads to restrictions (LinkedIn's stated policy, one press-confirmed case, many consistent but self-interested vendor accounts); weak on frequency; essentially nil on read-only job-search automation and on Indeed.
- The asymmetry matters for the decision: the user's tool only reads search results. That activity has (a) a fully functional logged-out path on both sites, and (b) no reported account casualties — while logging in adds risk to an asset the user cares about and, for search results alone, buys only personalisation/extra fields. Unless a needed feature is login-only, the evidence favours logged-out for both sites and reserving the real LinkedIn account for manual use.
- If logged-in LinkedIn access is ever needed, the least-bad form suggested by the evidence is the real account (not a second one), in the user's normal browser profile, at human-plausible volume (dozens, not hundreds, of searches per day), read-only, stopping immediately on any warning/CAPTCHA/restriction notice — accepting that this is still a breach and that a first flag typically means a temporary lockout and possibly an ID check.

### Gaps
