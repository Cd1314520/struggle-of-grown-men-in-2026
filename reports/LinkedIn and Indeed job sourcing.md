# Discover logged-out, apply on employer boards

Add both sites as logged-out, account-free discovery feeds and never as apply channels: Indeed through the mobile-app GraphQL endpoint that JobSpy and ts-jobspy use, LinkedIn through its guest job-search endpoint at one page per query, and every hit resolved to the employer's own ATS before anything is applied to. Indeed is the easy half: 100 results per request with full descriptions and usually an employer URL, no login, and no rate limit observed by maintainers as of this week. LinkedIn is the constrained half: 10 cards per request, a reported 429 after roughly ten requests in a burst from one IP, filters that are partly ignored, and, since mid-2026, **no external apply URL for logged-out visitors at all**. No account should be attached to the scanner on either site, and a dedicated second LinkedIn account is the worst choice available, because it breaks the one-account rule on top of the automation rule and mirrors the fact pattern LinkedIn litigates. Both sites' terms prohibit automated access outright, so this design is lower-risk, not permitted; the realistic downside when logged out is a temporary block on the home IP, not legal action or account loss. A scan of Houston / Austin costs about five to seven requests across both sites every 30 minutes, and the scanner should stop on the first block signal instead of rotating around it. The largest unknowns are sustained-polling tolerance on LinkedIn's guest endpoint, Bun compatibility of ts-jobspy, and how often a posting can actually be matched to an employer board; none of these was tested live beyond a single LinkedIn request.

## LinkedIn throttles guests near ten requests; Indeed's app API shows no limit yet

### LinkedIn logged out

The guest endpoint `https://www.linkedin.com/jobs-guest/jobs/api/seeMoreJobPostings/search` still works. A single plain `curl` from the target machine's residential IP on 2026-10-06, with no cookies or proxy, returned HTTP 200 and **10 job cards** ([endpoint](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs-guest/jobs/api/seeMoreJobPostings/search?keywords=software%20engineer&location=Austin%2C%20TX&f_TPR=r86400&start=0)). That matches JobSpy's constants of `jobs_per_page = 10` and `max_results = 1000` ([JobSpy constant.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/main/jobspy/linkedin/constant.py)) and contradicts vendor guides that say the guest endpoint pages in steps of 25 ([JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/linkedin-job-scraper-python)). The code should still count the cards on the first page and advance `start` by that count instead of hard-coding either number.

The only concrete rate-limit number in circulation is JobSpy's: "LinkedIn is the most restrictive and usually rate limits around the 10th page with one ip" ([JobSpy README](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy)). Every vendor figure traces back to that sentence, and it is old README text, not a 2026 measurement. JobSpy already sleeps a random 3 to 7 seconds between pages ([JobSpy linkedin module](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/blob/main/jobspy/linkedin/__init__.py)), so the trigger is roughly **ten requests inside 30 to 70 seconds**, and short delays alone do not prevent it. No source gives a per-hour or per-day ceiling, a block duration, or evidence that weeks of low-rate polling from one residential IP goes unnoticed. The block signals to code for are four: a 200 with an empty body (JobSpy keeps a sentinel for it), HTTP 429, LinkedIn's non-standard HTTP 999, and a redirect to a login wall ([JobSpy PR #404](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/pull/404), [http.dev/999](https://http.dev/999)). One browser-based scraper escalates its waits 5, 15, then 45 seconds after a 429, which suggests per-job throttles clear quickly, but that is one tool's heuristic and says nothing about IP-level blocks ([py-linkedin-jobs-scraper](https://github.com/spinlud/py-linkedin-jobs-scraper)). A more recent data point is encouraging without being conclusive: ts-jobspy reports a residential-IP benchmark on 2026-08-31 of about 157 LinkedIn jobs per minute with zero failures, on a 15-job sample ([ts-jobspy README](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/ts-jobspy#readme)).

### LinkedIn logged in

The commercial use limit is not the constraint. LinkedIn's help page lists "Searching for jobs on the Jobs page" among activities that do **not** count toward it, and publishes no numeric threshold ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a564226)). The constraint is automation detection on an account the user cares about, and no source gives a safe number of automated logged-in job searches per day; published "daily limits" from outreach-tool vendors concern connection requests and messages. Nothing reliable was found on whether LinkedIn detects CDP attachment to a real Chrome.

### Indeed

Indeed has two surfaces with opposite behavior. The website (`/jobs`, `/viewjob`) sits behind Cloudflare bot management and Turnstile; plain HTTP clients get a 403 "Security Check" page ([ScrapFly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-indeedcom), [Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/scrape-indeed-with-python)). The numbers available for it are vendor rules of thumb, not measurements: 3 to 6 seconds between requests, about 100 requests per IP per session ([Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/scrape-indeed-with-python)), and roughly 20% of search requests returning a 401 "Authenticating..." redirect even on residential sessions ([browse.sh](https://browse.sh/skills/indeed.com/search-jobs-8yxl6y)). No first-hand report states how many page loads a real Chrome on one home IP gets before a challenge.

The mobile-app API is the opposite. `POST https://apis.indeed.com/graphql` with an app key hard-coded in JobSpy's public source, iPhone "Indeed App" headers, and no cookie or login returns **100 results per request** with full HTML descriptions, capped near 1,000 per search ([JobSpy indeed constant.py](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/blob/main/jobspy/indeed/constant.py)). JobSpy's README calls it "the best scraper currently with no rate limiting", and the module contains no delay ([JobSpy README](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/blob/main/README.md)). The evidence is fresh: the maintainer confirmed "Indeed works today" on 2026-09-30 ([issue #287](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/287)), and a user fetched 150 results in 2.8 seconds from a GitHub Actions datacenter runner on 2026-10-03 ([issue #385](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/385)). "No rate limiting" is maintainer experience, not a measured quota, and the route hangs on one shared key that Indeed can revoke at any time. Logging in buys nothing on Indeed: search is readable logged out ([ScrapFly](https://scrapfly.io/blog/posts/how-to-scrape-indeedcom)), and no source says a signed-in session is challenged less.

## No account belongs in the scanner, and a second LinkedIn account is the worst option

LinkedIn's User Agreement (effective November 3, 2025) has no carve-out for read-only, personal, or low-volume use. Section 8.2 forbids using "software, devices, scripts, robots or any other means or processes" to scrape, and "bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services"; section 1.2 applies the contract to logged-out "Visitors" as well as Members ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). Its robots.txt disallows everything for unlisted agents ([robots.txt](https://www.linkedin.com/robots.txt)). Indeed's Terms, updated September 29, 2026, prohibit "any automated system (bots, scrapers, spiders, AI or Agentic AI)" without written permission and bind anyone "accessing or using" the site, including through an API; they also forbid access "through any means other than the public interfaces provided by Indeed", which squarely covers the mobile-app GraphQL route ([Indeed Terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)). The scanner breaches both sets of terms as written. The question is consequence, not permission.

A dedicated LinkedIn account makes things worse. Section 2.1 says "you will only have one LinkedIn account, which must be in your real name", so a second account adds a violation, and a pseudonymous one adds the false-identity clause on top ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement)). Fake accounts are the conduct in every LinkedIn scraping suit found: hiQ's consent judgment, Proxycurl in 2025, ProAPIs in 2025 ([National Law Review](https://www.natlawreview.com/article/linkedin-s-data-scraping-battle-hiq-labs-ends-proposed-judgment), [The Record](https://therecord.media/linkedin-sues-data-scraping-company)). A restricted account is routed to Persona government-ID verification where the ID name must match the profile, which a pseudonymous account cannot pass and a real-name duplicate exposes ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1342692)). Vendor blogs claim LinkedIn links accounts by device, IP, and phone and restricts both; that is plausible from a single home IP but rests on no primary evidence ([evaboot](https://evaboot.com/blog/how-to-manage-multiple-linkedin-accounts)). Indeed's terms likewise say "You may not create multiple accounts" ([Indeed Terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)).

The real ban risk sorts by what is exposed. Logged out, there is no account to restrict; the documented consequences are 429s and IP blocks. With the real account, LinkedIn's own help page describes a first automation flag as a time-limited restriction that "will then automatically be re-enabled at the time specified on the suspension notification" once the tool is disabled ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340567)). Every documented restriction involves high-volume write actions or profile scraping; the best-attested case is the AIHawk developer's account, restricted in September 2024 after the tool drove hundreds of Easy Apply submissions per user ([Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/free-ai-tool-help-apply-110301118.html)). No report was found of an account restricted for low-volume read-only job search, and none confirming it is safe; quiet users do not file reports. One open question matters for this setup: no source says whether LinkedIn ties logged-out traffic to a logged-in account on the same IP. The cheap precaution is to run the scanner from a cookie-less HTTP client or a separate Chrome profile that has never held a LinkedIn or Indeed session, and to accept that an IP-level block would add friction to the user's own manual browsing.

On the law (not legal advice): the Supreme Court in Van Buren rejected the theory that violating a use policy alone is a federal computer crime ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2021/2021-06-09-us-supreme-court-computer-fraud-abuse-act-van-buren)), and the Ninth Circuit in hiQ held the CFAA's "without authorization" concept does not reach public pages ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2022/12/linkedin-v-hiq-landmark-data-scraping-suit-provides-guidance-to-data-scrapers-and-web-operators)). Contract claims failed against a logged-out scraper in Meta v. Bright Data and for lack of damage in X Corp. v. Bright Data ([Eric Goldman's blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/archives/2024/01/game-on-bright-data-scores-major-victory-in-web-scraping-dispute-with-meta-guest-blog-post.htm), [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/395944/judge-throws-out-xs-claims-against-data-scraper.html)). LinkedIn's and Indeed's terms are drafted more broadly than Meta's, so that result does not transfer automatically, and hiQ on remand shows anti-scraping terms are enforceable against a party that accepted them. No lawsuit, demand letter, or cease-and-desist against an individual job seeker was found from either company. What changes the picture is circumvention: rotating proxies, CAPTCHA solvers, and fingerprint spoofing after a block are the facts "bypass or circumvent any access controls" is written for. Backing off keeps the conduct in the benign category, which is why this design uses no proxies.

One sanctioned route exists. Indeed's MCP connector lets an AI assistant search jobs and return "application URLs" after an Indeed sign-in, and Indeed's terms name such "Permitted AI Connector" integrations as the approved path for AI access ([Indeed MCP docs](https://docs.indeed.com/mcp), [Indeed Terms](https://www.indeed.com/legal)). It is documented for Claude.ai and Claude Desktop only, publishes no rate limits, and exposes no recency or radius filter, so it does not fit a headless 30-minute scan. LinkedIn has nothing comparable: its Job Posting API is partner-only, write-only, and "currently not accepting new partnerships" ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/talent/job-postings/api/overview)). Indeed's Publisher API is gone, with sources disagreeing on whether it ended in 2020 or 2023 ([Thunderbit](https://thunderbit.com/blog/scrape-indeed-with-python), [JobsPipe](https://jobspipe.dev/blog/indeed-publisher-api)).

## ts-jobspy leads a consolidated toolchain

The open-source field has collapsed onto JobSpy and its ports. JobFunnel is archived ([GitHub](https://github.com/PaulMcInnis/JobFunnel)), `tomquirk/linkedin-api` returns 404 ([GitHub](https://github.com/tomquirk/linkedin-api)), the AIHawk repository has been repurposed into an unrelated browser tool ([GitHub](https://github.com/feder-cr/Jobs_Applier_AI_Agent_AIHawk)), and spinlud's `linkedin-jobs-scraper` v19 deleted its anonymous mode and now requires a LinkedIn session ([spinlud README](https://github.com/spinlud/linkedin-jobs-scraper#readme)). JobSpy itself went 18 months without a release, during which several boards broke silently, then shipped v1.2.0 on 2026-10-02 and merged LinkedIn and Indeed fixes on 2026-10-06 that are on `main` but not yet on PyPI ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/pypi/python-jobspy/json), [commits](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/commits/main)). Its health depends on one maintainer's availability, so the caller must distinguish "zero jobs" from "error".

ts-jobspy is the easiest call from Bun on paper: one `scrapeJobs({ sites, location, distance, hoursOld, siteConcurrency: 1 })` call, with a `meta.sites[].status` of `ok`, `empty`, `partial`, or `error` that surfaces a 429 as a `RateLimitException` without auto-retrying, which is exactly what a poller needs ([ts-jobspy README](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/ts-jobspy#readme)). Its weaknesses are a single maintainer, an undici dependency chain never documented under Bun, and Node's TLS stack, which its own README says "sophisticated anti-bot systems can still fingerprint". It also sends only one Indeed filter group per search (`hoursOld`, or job type and remote, or Easy Apply), a limitation Python JobSpy fixed on 2026-09-30 ([JobSpy PR #392](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/pull/392)). Python JobSpy is the hedge: the box already has `uv`, Python 3.12, and Bun, so a subprocess needs no global install. Because the two endpoints are simple, a third option is to write both fetchers directly in TypeScript using JobSpy's source as the reference; that removes the dependency risk at the cost of owning breakage.

Hosted fallbacks keep the home IP and any account out of the picture, but two cautions apply. Pay-per-result billing punishes polling: an actor that re-returns the same newest jobs three times an hour bills for every repeat, so LinkedIn actors belong on demand or behind a tight posted-within filter. And Apify listings that advertise a LinkedIn "direct apply URL" ([bebity actor](https://apify.com/bebity/linkedin-jobs-scraper)) conflict with a JobSpy user's July 2026 report that their Apify fallback for that field stopped working too ([JobSpy issue #370](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/370)). Direct Google Jobs scraping is dead: Google serves a JavaScript-required shell to non-browser clients ([JobSpy issue #284](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/284)).

## Houston first costs five to seven requests per scan; other metros rotate in

### Search parameters

For LinkedIn, current JobSpy sends `keywords`, `location`, `distance` (miles), `start`, and `f_TPR=r<seconds>` to the guest endpoint, so those are confirmed in use ([JobSpy linkedin module](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/blob/main/jobspy/linkedin/__init__.py)). The recency window takes arbitrary seconds: `r3600` is one hour, `r86400` a day ([Kondo](https://www.trykondo.com/blog/linkedin-url-job-hack)). Start with `location=Houston, TX`, `distance=25`, and `f_TPR=r7200` on a 30-minute cadence so consecutive windows overlap, then dedupe on the job ID from the card. A numeric `geoId` disambiguates the location, but Houston's value was not found in any source; read it once from the browser URL after picking the location in LinkedIn's dropdown. Do not rely on server-side seniority, job-type, or remote filters. JobSpy's 2026-10-06 fix stops sending `is_remote` and `job_type` because "the logged-out search ignores both" ([PR #404](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/pull/404)), and an Apify maintainer notes LinkedIn discontinued experience level and job type as separate URL parameters when it moved to AI-powered search ([curious_coder actor](https://apify.com/curious_coder/linkedin-jobs-scraper)). Filter client-side on the card title instead, dropping "Senior", "Staff", "Principal", and similar. `sortBy=DD` (newest first) and `f_E` (2 entry, 3 associate, 4 mid-senior) are documented for the logged-in URL only and are untested on the guest endpoint ([Kondo](https://www.trykondo.com/blog/linkedin-url-job-hack)).

For Indeed's GraphQL route the equivalents are `what`, `location { where, radius, radiusUnit: MILES }` with an arbitrary integer radius, and a date filter on `dateOnIndeed` in hours ([JobSpy indeed constant.py](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/blob/main/jobspy/indeed/constant.py)). Use `where: "Houston, TX"`, radius 25 to 35, and a 2-hour window per scan with one 24-hour sweep per day. Matching is strict: every query word must appear in the title or description, quotes give exact phrases, `-word` excludes, and `OR` groups work ([JobSpy issue #229](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/229)). Sort is hard-coded to relevance, so freshness comes from the date filter. Dedupe on the job `key`. If the web route is ever needed, the parameters are `q`, `l`, `radius` (0 to 100), `fromage=1`, `sort=date`, and `start` in steps of 10, with allowed values resting on one undated third-party page ([browse.sh](https://browse.sh/skills/indeed.com/search-jobs-8yxl6y)).

### Request budget

The arithmetic below is design reasoning from the cited page sizes, not a reported safe limit. Three LinkedIn keyword queries (for example "software engineer", "backend engineer", "full stack developer") at one page each is **3 guest requests per scan**, spaced 10 to 30 seconds apart with jitter, or about 144 a day. Two to four Indeed queries at one POST each is **2 to 4 requests per scan**, about 100 to 200 a day, with zero detail fetches because descriptions arrive in the response. LinkedIn descriptions cost one extra request per job in every scraper examined ([ts-jobspy README](https://github.com/alpharomercoma/ts-jobspy#readme)), and here the resolution-first design pays off: the card already carries title, company, and location, which is enough to look the job up on the employer's ATS and read the description there. Reserve LinkedIn detail fetches for postings that fail resolution, and cap them at a handful per scan, drip-fed. Backfill is the dangerous moment; a first run asking for 24 hours across several pages plus descriptions would exceed ten requests in a minute, so spread it at one page per cycle.

### Expansion

Houston runs every scan. Other metros join a round-robin of one metro per scan, so LinkedIn never exceeds about six search requests per 30-minute window regardless of how many cities are configured; five metros at three queries each would otherwise reach 15 requests per scan, the size of the burst reported to trigger a 429. Expand on Indeed first, where each metro costs 2 to 4 POSTs and no limit has been observed. Gate LinkedIn expansion on evidence: add a metro only after a run of days with no 429, 999, empty-body, or login-wall response, and drop back to Houston alone on the first one. On any block signal, stop the LinkedIn scan, skip at least the next cycle, and back off exponentially. Never retry in a loop and never change IP.

## LinkedIn hides the employer link from guests, so resolution runs through the ATS

How reliably each site exposes the external apply URL splits cleanly. On LinkedIn the answer is now never when logged out. JobSpy's maintainer closed the issue on 2026-09-30: "LinkedIn no longer includes the external apply URL for logged-out visitors. The job page has no apply link, and clicking Apply only opens a sign-in prompt", and removed the parser ([JobSpy issue #370](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/issues/370)). Documentation that still promises a "direct application URL, if available" in anonymous mode predates the change ([jobspy-js docs](https://mintlify.wiki/borgius/jobspy-js/job-boards/linkedin)). The only open-source tool still returning a LinkedIn apply link does so by logging in ([spinlud README](https://github.com/spinlud/linkedin-jobs-scraper#readme)).

Indeed does expose it. The GraphQL response carries `recruit.viewJobUrl`, which JobSpy returns as `job_url_direct` unless the host is indeed.com, in which case the job is Indeed-hosted or Indeed Apply ([JobSpy PR #403](https://github.com/speedyapply/JobSpy/pull/403)). The web route's `thirdPartyApplyUrl` is typically an Indeed `/applystart` redirect needing one more Cloudflare-fronted request to resolve ([Apify dltik actor](https://apify.com/dltik/indeed-jobs-scraper)), another reason to prefer GraphQL. How often `viewJobUrl` is a final ATS link, a tracking link, or missing is not documented. The only share figure is Indeed's FY2023 statement that fewer than 50% of jobs are Indeed Apply-enabled, across all job categories ([Recruit Holdings](https://investorupdate-fy23.recruit-holdings.com/pdf/en/day1/investor_update_fy2023_day1_simplify_hiring_presentation_2_en.pdf)). For LinkedIn, the Easy Apply share estimates found were roughly 40% and roughly 10%, from marketing blogs that contradict each other ([FastApply blog](https://blog.fastapply.co/linkedin-easy-apply-vs-direct-company-applications-2026)); treat the split as unknown and measure it.

The user's preference to apply on the company site therefore needs a resolver, and the cheapest steps reuse what the pipeline already has. First, when Indeed supplies a non-Indeed URL, follow redirects and classify the final host. Second, look the company up in a permanent local cache of company to ATS and slug; an open, Common Crawl-derived list of about 95,000 company identifiers across Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, and others can seed it, under a CC BY-NC licence that suits personal use ([Feashliaa/job-board-aggregator](https://github.com/Feashliaa/job-board-aggregator)). Third, probe normalized name guesses against the public no-auth endpoints the pipeline already reads: `boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs` ([Greenhouse docs](https://docs.greenhouse.io/job-board.html)), `api.lever.co/v0/postings/{slug}?mode=json`, and `api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{name}` ([Fantastic.jobs](https://fantastic.jobs/article/ats-with-api)). Fourth, fetch the company's `/careers` or `/jobs` page and look for known ATS hostnames, falling back to the real Chrome over CDP when the board renders client-side. One commercial resolver using this probe-then-parse cascade reports 10 of 13 domains resolved, all correctly; that is a vendor self-report on a tiny sample ([Apify fayoussef actor](https://apify.com/fayoussef/company-domain-to-job-postings)).

Workday needs special handling because its tenant, shard, and site "cannot be guessed" and must be lifted from an existing job link. Once known, `POST https://{tenant}.wd{N}.myworkdayjobs.com/wday/cxs/{tenant}/{site}/jobs` accepts a `searchText` and a hard `limit` of 20 ([dev.to Workday write-up](https://dev.to/udaninn/workday-job-boards-have-a-json-api-too-its-just-better-hidden-23fl)). The source of that link, and the fallback for iCIMS, Taleo, and other boards with no uniform public feed, is a targeted lookup against Google for Jobs data. SerpApi's `google_jobs` engine and JSearch both return an `apply_options` list of titled links per job ([SerpApi docs](https://serpapi.com/google-jobs-api), [JSearch](https://www.openwebninja.com/api/jsearch)); pick the entry whose host is a known ATS domain or the company's own. Free quotas of 250 and 200 a month cover on-demand use for shortlisted jobs, not polling, and SerpApi's 2026 release notes record intermittent empty results, so an empty answer means "unknown", not "no job" ([SerpApi release notes](https://serpapi.com/google-jobs-api/release-notes)). A general web search with `site:` operators is the remaining option; Google's Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers and ends January 1, 2027 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview)), so that means a third-party SERP API.

Two checks keep resolution honest. Verify ownership after any slug guess, because short generic slugs collide: the resolver above found correct boards mentioned the company's domain 32 to 840 times and wrong boards zero times ([Apify fayoussef actor](https://apify.com/fayoussef/company-domain-to-job-postings)). Then match the posting: prefer a requisition or job ID when an apply URL carries one, otherwise normalized company plus token-sorted fuzzy title plus compatible location, since exact title matching "caught nothing" in one practitioner's test ([dev.to dedup write-up](https://dev.to/imapphelp/my-job-search-api-returned-the-same-job-five-times-the-apply-url-gave-away-the-dedup-key-1fj2)). No precision or recall figures exist for any matching scheme. Absence from the live employer board is itself useful: it flags closed listings, reposters, and staffing-agency adverts, and those should be marked unresolvable and left for the user, not applied to through Easy Apply.

## The lowest-risk design, and what a live test must settle first

The recommendation is a two-site discovery layer that never holds a session. Indeed runs through the GraphQL route every scan and is treated as the primary new source, because it is cheap, fast, and returns descriptions and employer links. LinkedIn runs through the guest endpoint at three single-page queries per scan, cards only, from a cookie-less client kept apart from the Chrome profile the user browses with. Both feed one resolver that maps each posting to an employer board and hands it to the existing Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby application path; unresolved postings go to a manual review queue. ts-jobspy is the first implementation to try, Python JobSpy via `uv` the fallback behind the same interface, Apify's pay-per-result actors the escape hatch if the home IP is throttled, and JSearch or SerpApi the on-demand apply-link resolver. No proxies, no CAPTCHA solving, no logged-in automation, no second account. If a LinkedIn apply link is ever essential for one shortlisted job, the user clicks Apply by hand in their normal browser.

The steelman for logging in deserves a fair hearing: job search is exempt from LinkedIn's commercial use limit, a first automation flag usually auto-lifts, and a signed-in session restores the apply link and working filters. It still loses, because the search results themselves are available logged out, the employer link is recoverable by other means, and the account is the one asset here that cannot be replaced during an active job search.

Almost everything above is desk research. The only live observation is one LinkedIn guest request. These tests should run before any real build effort:

| Test before building | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Import ts-jobspy under Bun and run one Indeed and one LinkedIn query | Bun compatibility is undocumented; decides native import versus `uv` subprocess versus hand-written fetchers |
| One Indeed GraphQL POST for Houston from the home IP | The route was confirmed from other networks this week, never from this machine |
| Share of Indeed results with a non-Indeed `viewJobUrl`, and where those URLs land | Determines how much resolution work Indeed needs; no published figure for software roles |
| LinkedIn guest behavior of `f_TPR=r3600` or `r7200`, `sortBy=DD`, `f_E`, `geoId`, and `distance` values | All documented for the logged-in URL or by a single scraper; guest behavior unconfirmed |
| A week of LinkedIn polling at 3 requests per 30 minutes with every status code logged | No source covers sustained low-rate polling from one residential IP, or block duration |
| Open three to five known offsite-apply LinkedIn postings logged out | Confirms the apply link is really gone from both the guest fragment and the public job page |
| Count new Houston job IDs per day per query on each site | No posting-volume data exists; sizes the detail-fetch and resolver budgets |
| Resolver hit rate on about 50 real postings, by cascade step | The only published figure is a vendor's 10 of 13; this decides whether the fallback is worth building at all |
| Read the full Indeed robots.txt and current vendor pricing pages | Only the first 60 lines of robots.txt were read; several prices came from third-party trackers |

Other points remain unknown and cannot be settled by a quick test: whether LinkedIn links logged-out traffic to an account on the same IP, whether Indeed's app key carries a hidden quota or will be revoked, whether the Indeed RSS feed still responds, and what share of LinkedIn software postings are Easy Apply only or agency reposts.

## Conclusion

The research inverts the usual framing of LinkedIn as the prize and Indeed as the afterthought. For this pipeline Indeed is the richer feed per request, and LinkedIn's guest surface in 2026 is closer to a list of company names and titles than a job source: no apply link, unreliable filters, ten cards at a time. That makes LinkedIn most valuable as a company discovery signal. Every Houston employer it surfaces whose board resolves to Greenhouse, Lever, or Ashby can be added to the board-polling list the pipeline already runs, after which that company's future jobs arrive first-hand, with no LinkedIn request and no terms exposure. The cache of resolved companies is the durable asset; the scrapers are a disposable way to grow it.

That also sets the right expectation for durability. The Indeed route hangs on one shared app key and the LinkedIn route on an undocumented endpoint whose useful fields have already been cut once this year, so both should be built as replaceable adapters behind a status-reporting interface, with success measured by how many postings reach an employer board, not by how many cards are scraped. If the one-week live test shows blocks at three requests per scan, the fallback is not proxies or a login; it is Apify's free credit for LinkedIn and a heavier lean on Indeed and direct ATS feeds.
