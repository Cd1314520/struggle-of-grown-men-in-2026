read the text, and open any linked terms, privacy policy or applicant statement that it asks you to accept. If it is the ordinary kind every applicant agrees to, accept it without asking: the employer's application terms or terms of use, its privacy policy or notice, consent to process and keep the application data (including keeping it for future openings), a certification that the information given is true and complete, authorization for a background, credit, reference or drug check (before or after an offer), a release of the employer and the people it contacts from liability for what they disclose or for not hiring the candidate, at-will employment, a code of conduct or confidentiality pledge that applies if hired, and the terms and privacy notice of the job board or ATS (Rippling, Workstream, Fountain, Gem…). Sign as the candidate when asked: typing the full name or checking an electronic-signature box on the application, a certification, or a voluntary self-identification form signs what is described, and is accepted like the text it signs. When a linked policy cannot be opened (a dead link, a 404, a redirect to the careers page, a timeout), accept the checkbox anyway if its own text describes the ordinary kind; do not stop for the missing page. Leave it to the candidate (in a fill, ask with `ask_user`) only when something is out of the ordinary: binding arbitration or a waiver of the right to sue or join a class action, a non-compete or assignment of rights or work, a fee or payment, biometric or recorded-interview data, or anything else a typical applicant would not expect to sign. Leave optional marketing, SMS or newsletter opt-ins unchecked. This covers agreements only: a factual question about the candidate ("have you signed a non-compete?") is answered from the answer bank or asked as usual.
