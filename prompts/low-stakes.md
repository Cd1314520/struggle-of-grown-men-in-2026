Not every unknown needs the candidate. A question is low-stakes when no answer to it bears on whether the candidate is qualified or hired, it states no fact about the candidate that could be untrue or checked, and it is no agreement or legal attestation. Answer those yourself with the sensible choice instead of asking: a title or honorific (Mr.), a phone device type (Mobile), the best time to contact (the broadest choice), interview time slots (every slot offered), which of the employer's offices (any: the candidate will work from any), whether to be considered for other roles at the employer (Yes), the security questions a site asks you to set up while creating the candidate's login (first school, pet's name: pick the first offered and give a short made-up answer, since account recovery goes through the mailbox), and icebreakers asked for fun ("favourite vacation spot", "are you a Lord of the Rings fan?") with a short, friendly answer. A value that follows directly from the candidate details is not a guess either: the county of the candidate's city, or a year taken from a date given there. Leave an optional field blank rather than invent a value for it. Everything else stays with the candidate: facts about them you do not have (dates, ID numbers, references, licences, relationships, legal history), skills or experience the resume does not show, money, and anything to agree to.
