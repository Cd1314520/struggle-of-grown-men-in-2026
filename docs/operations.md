# Operating it

What the app does on its own, where it stops for you, and how to bring a stopped application back.

## Approval and target employers

Two settings in `config.yml` decide what the app does on its own, and Settings → General changes both, writing to `config.local.yml`:

- **`approval`**: `auto` lets the app approve the jobs that clear the `autoApply` bar (gate 1) and send every application without asking again (gate 2). `gated` stops every job at both gates for you. Jobs you add or send back by hand, and questions the agent cannot answer, wait for you either way.
- **`targets`**: the employers you are saving for later. `describe` says in words what a target employer is; the prep worker judges every employer against it (`target: yes | no`, with the sentence behind it). The `frontier` list in `profile/employers.yml` is always a target and the `practice` list never is, overriding the judgment. Empty `describe`: the frontier list alone. With `holdBack: true`, jobs at targets are held (Archive → Held) and everything else is applied to as practice. With `holdBack: false`, targets are applied to as well. Turning hold-back off in Settings releases the jobs held at targets back through triage; jobs you held yourself stay held.

Each job is scored on the rubric for its employer: `real` in `profile/rubric.yml` for a target, `practice` for any other. Both rubrics are answered in every assessment. A target that passes the real rubric is held or applied to as `holdBack` says; one that fails it is skipped, so a weak practice fit never throws away a job the real search wants. A practice job that fails its qualifications or interview fit is skipped. Pay and location do not decide what is a target. The worker also records whether the engineering is **agent-native** (`yes`, `no`, `unclear`), which decides nothing on its own.

Each employer is judged once per description. While targets are held back, a job at an employer an earlier full assessment judged a target (or on the frontier list) only gets the screen for the real rubric's plain requirements, then is held without a full assessment. `rememberedEmployer` in `src/employers.ts` reads the earlier judgments, counting only those made against the current `describe` (a fingerprint of its words is stored with each, `against`); one target judgment outranks the rest. Changing the description therefore judges employers afresh as their jobs come in. Jobs already held keep their judgment until released. A job you pick out by hand is always assessed in full.

The prep worker stores a versioned `practice_assessment` (version 4). Version 3 named one of five employer types; it reads as a judgment against the default description, a tech company at scale being the one target, so it carries over while `describe` is unchanged. Versions 1 and 2 judged pay and location and read as not judged. The deterministic gate checks the judgment, not its truth. The rule is `practiceVerdict` in `src/practice.ts`; `src/config.ts` reads the settings from before (`phase`, `confirmBeforeSubmit`, `autoApply.enabled`/`submit`/`phases`) as the new ones.

`autoApply` sets the bar for the app's own approvals: the fit score a job needs (`minScore`), whether concerns may stand (`allowConcerns`), how many it may approve a day (`perDay`), and per employer, ever (`perEmployer`). `limits.applyPerDay` caps application attempts and `limits.prepPerDay` caps assessments; `concurrency` sets the number of prep workers and browser slots; `models` names the model for each stage. Only a new application consumes `applyPerDay` quota; resuming one already started does not. New applications are spread out: one starts at most every `limits.applySpreadHours` / `applyPerDay` (24 hours by default), so the cap is used through the day rather than in a burst after the reset. A posting opened within the last day is not held by that gap: it starts as soon as a browser is free, and the approved posting opened most recently always goes first. Resumes and verifications are not paced. Counters reset at midnight UTC.

Gate 1 by auto apply requires a freshly passing assessment, no unclear required answers on the job's rubric, a score of at least `minScore`, and remaining `perDay` capacity. While targets are held back the employer must be proven not a target; otherwise it must at least have been judged. Hand-picked jobs and jobs sent back with feedback are not auto-approved. `allowConcerns: true` lets ordinary concerns stand; it does not override required qualifications. Rules live in `src/practice.ts`, `src/rubric.ts`, and `src/auto.ts`; `approved_by` and Activity record auto-approvals.

`autoApply.perEmployer` caps how many applications auto apply makes to one employer, ever: once an employer has that many sent or under way, a further role there that passes waits in review with a note saying so. When the app starts, auto-approved jobs not yet started that are over the allowance go back to review, keeping each employer's best-scored role when none has started. Roles you approve yourself are never trimmed, though they count toward the allowance.

Jobs blocked **only by the auto-approval daily cap** carry a `deferred_at` timestamp on the job. The scheduler rechecks them when capacity becomes available, including after the UTC reset or a configured cap increase. Reconsideration uses the current rubric, stored practice evidence, feedback protection, and the existing tailored PDF; approval and quota consumption happen together. Ordinary manual-review jobs are not swept into automation.

While targets are held back, auto-approved jobs are checked again before resume freeze or browser startup, and when the app starts: missing, invalid or non-qualifying evidence puts them on hold with the reason, and one whose stored assessment lacks a judgment now asked for goes back to prep. An application already under way that only lacks the judgment carries on filling, but stops for your confirmation before it is submitted. Application answers remain truthful, including relocation preferences.

To reassess previously scored backlog, stop the service, run `bun run reassess-backlog` for a read-only preview, then `bun run reassess-backlog --apply` and restart it. The command requeues old scored `awaiting_approval`, `held`, and `skipped` jobs for fresh prep; it does not approve or submit them directly. Rejected, active, submitted, manually approved, held-by-you, feedback-marked, frozen-resume, and already-current assessments are preserved. Prep and application daily caps still apply.

Only the fill worker has a browser, and it only starts after an approval, so gate 1 is structural. Every worker is instructed to call `confirm_submit` before the final click. That host tool reads the current job and immediately authorizes eligible automatic submissions; jobs requiring confirmation park for your decision. Manual final approval is application-scoped, exact-summary and one-use; changed answers or a changed summary require review again. The restart-protection marker is set only when final submission is authorized, not when filling begins. The browser itself cannot enforce use of the tool.

## Prefill

Before the fill agent reads a page of an application form, it runs `src/prefill.ts` in its own JavaScript session (prompts/fill.md says how). The script fills what the candidate details settle with no judgment: name, contact details, address, links, current job, school, the resume upload, and the plain work-authorisation, sponsorship, age, EEO, referral, source and text-message questions, whose answers are the `prefill:` block of `profile/personal.yml` (a question it has no answer for there is left alone). It clicks and types one field at a time through the same paced browser as the agent, corrects plain text a site filled in from the resume, reads every field back once the page settles, and prints a short report of what it filled and what it left. The agent then spends its turns only on the fields left. Fields revealed by an answer (race after "not Hispanic") are picked up in the same call; a field that wants one of its own suggestions picked (Lever's location) is reported as not having stuck. It reads the top-level page only, so a form inside a frame is opened at the frame's own address first. `fill.ts` writes the facts it reads to `data/jobs/<id>/prefill.json` when each fill starts.

## Parked applications and Questions

Unknown required facts, required cover letters and final-review decisions are durable gates, not workers waiting on an in-memory promise. The gate and browser/draft checkpoint are saved before the worker stops; its ordinary browser slot becomes available only after the process exits. Restarting the app preserves the wait rather than replaying the application. A gate stays open only while its application is parked (`needs_input`, `awaiting_submit`, `needs_manual`): any move out of those statuses closes its open gates and releases a browser reservation, so a gate never outlives the wait it describes.

Use **Questions** to handle the backlog in batches. An answer goes to the chosen application and, while **Also add it to your answers** stays ticked (the default for questions), to every listed application asking exactly the same thing, and into the answer bank, `profile/answers.md`. The bank agent (`src/answer-bank.ts`, `prompts/answer-bank.md`, run on the `recheck` model) works out what each kept answer says beyond its form and edits the file to say it: widening an entry already there, correcting one the answer contradicts, or adding one; an answer that holds only for one employer goes in naming it, or not at all. `answers.md` as it stood before each run that changed it is kept in `data/answer-bank/`. The recheck then answers the parked questions the bank now settles, at any employer and in any wording. Untick it for an answer that holds only for that application. Saving an answer requeues the resolved applications automatically, subject to daily limits, pacing and availability of their original browser/profile.

Interview prep (`src/interview-prep.ts`, `prompts/interview-prep.md`, run on the `interview` model) writes a markdown brief for an open `interview` step (to do, or booked for a time that has not passed) of a sent application: what the round likely is, topics to brush up on ranked against the posting and the assessment's gaps, likely questions tied to the resume, questions to ask, and company facts found by web search with their source. The scheduler runs it from its 30-second tick and right after each mailbox pass, up to 3 steps per run, one worker run per step, each step at most twice. It has no daily limit and, like the other workers, waits out a provider backoff. The brief shows on the step under **Interview prep**; **Refresh prep** in the step's menu writes it again.

The recheck (`src/recheck.ts`, `prompts/recheck.md`, run on the `recheck` model) reads the personal details, the answer bank, the stock resume and `profile/resume-notes.md`. Like the fill worker, which also gets the resume notes, it answers a yes/no or rating question about a skill, language, tool or kind of experience from the resume and the notes (yes when either shows it, no when neither does), so a fact kept only in the notes, such as Agile sprints in Jira, still counts; it writes a concise, honest answer to a free-text question from the resume and the question's context, and leaves unknown facts, agreements beyond the ordinary terms and ID numbers. It runs when the details, bank, resume, notes or its prompts change (a fingerprint, after a five-minute settle), and also for open questions it has not considered yet: a question parked for five minutes since the last completed run is rechecked once, and then not again until the fingerprint changes. A batch that does not finish is retried within the hour. For every question it answers, the application's activity log shows `Answered from the profile` with the basis; for every question it leaves, `Left for you` with the reason it gave, since the model must call `leave_question` for each one before it can finish.

When the fill worker asks a question it must say why it could not answer it itself (`why` on `ask_user`: `agreement`, `fact_missing`, `skill_not_on_resume`, `free_text`, `sensitive_id`, `history_detail` or `other`) and may name the candidate detail or answer bank entry that would settle it (`missing`). Both are stored on the gate (`question_gates.why` and `question_gates.missing`; gates from before this are blank) and shown as a short label and a "Missing:" hint on the Questions rows, the open question, and the application's decision card. A queued question keeps its own pair until it is parked, so each question of a batch shows its own reason.

Resumption uses the same frozen resume and verifies the exact employer/account/requisition and existing draft. A checkpoint does not prove the ATS saved every field. Missing facts are never guessed, and failure to establish draft identity requires manual inspection rather than starting a duplicate application.

**Manual handoffs and ambiguous submissions also release capacity:** once their worker exits, neither the saved gate nor its queued recovery reserves a browser slot. They do hold their employer for new applications: while an employer has an application started and unfinished (parked on any gate, or queued to resume its draft), no new application to it starts, so one site quirk or unanswered question stalls one application instead of every role there. Draft tabs, checkpoints, frozen resumes, answers, and submission protection remain intact. Multiple parked applications can share a persistent profile; their drafts stay isolated. **Check status & recover** requests one recovery attempt in the application's original browser/profile without claiming you fixed the problem. Recovery gets priority when runnable, but an unavailable profile or employer cannot block other eligible jobs. A pre-submit manual handoff resumes under the usual approval rules. A potentially submitted application instead gets a **verification-only worker, limited to ten minutes**: it can sign in using saved credentials and check the exact requisition and confirmation mail, but cannot obtain submission authorization.

Verification requires positive, job-specific evidence: a submitted portal status or matching confirmation email; or an explicit unsubmitted/incomplete draft or rejected submission. An empty inbox, login failure, or merely seeing a form is not proof of non-submission. Confirmed drafts resume with a fresh `confirm_submit` check; inconclusive checks preserve the submission protection and return a specific handoff, without looping automatically. Browser access is revoked before handoff and the slot is not reused until the worker exits. Status checks can run even when the new-application daily quota is exhausted; resumed filling still respects that quota. Restarting an unfinished verification returns it to inspection, never blind submission.

Conclusive `report_result` outcomes commit the terminal status, gate cancellation and cleared submission/recovery markers atomically, then revoke browser access and stop the worker. The browser slot and employer lock remain occupied until the process exits; saved tabs and frozen resumes remain intact. A failure after final-submit authorization still parks for evidence-based verification rather than clearing submission protection.

The API is `POST /api/jobs/:id/recover` with `{ "gateId": "<current gate id>" }`; stale gates and duplicate/active-worker requests are rejected. The UI retains manual inspection and explicit submitted/not-submitted resolutions. Answering a question cannot clear submission protection. Handoffs display the actual recorded failure, including reasons hidden in older ambiguity records.

For hands-on inspection, choose **Reserve browser for inspection**, then **Inspect reserved browser**. This is the only stopped-job action that reserves capacity, until **Release browser**, recovery, return-control, or resolution. Reservation requires the original slot and employer account to be idle, including worker teardown; it activates only the saved application tab and fails if that tab is missing. Release leaves the blocker, draft, and submission protection unchanged. Explicit reservations survive restart; old unreserved handoffs do not acquire a lease. The API is `POST /api/jobs/:id/browser` with `{ "action": "reserve" | "release", "gateId": "<current gate id>" }`; busy/stale requests return 409.

Recovery workers receive the oldest two and newest four manual/ambiguity handoffs, bounded to six records. These are historical evidence, not binding instructions: an intermediate worker's “original account required” assumption must not override the original record showing an outage before registration. Rejected verification codes trigger inspection for a supported resend flow rather than repeated use of the same stale code; this never authorizes another final-submit click while its outcome is unknown.

During a normal fill's just-authorized live attempt, an immediate CAPTCHA or email-code dialog is a continuation of that attempt. Workers may use clearly identified challenge controls, retrieve a fresh employer-matched code, and request a supported resend after rejection. They never click final application Submit twice. Repeated challenge failures, cleared forms or ambiguous controls park with specific evidence. Verification-only recovery workers may inspect status but must not complete a pending submission challenge.

## Model provider capacity

When a worker is refused because the model provider is at capacity or rate limited (`isCapacityError` in `src/errors.ts`), the scheduler starts no new agent work (scans, tracking, assessments, applications) for 2 minutes, doubling with each further refusal up to an hour; the first worker that completes clears both the backoff level and its deadline. A refusal that comes more than 6 hours after the last backoff ended starts again at 2 minutes. The refused work is not lost: an assessment goes back to `triaged` and its daily count is given back; a fill stopped before final submit was authorized resumes the same draft; a verification worker runs again. A fill stopped after authorization still parks as an ambiguous submission. At startup, assessments marked failed and pre-submit manual handoffs caused by a capacity refusal are requeued the same way.

The scheduler's tick runs every 30 seconds from the moment the service starts, after the startup recovery above, and stops when the service stops; starting it twice or stopping it when it is not running does nothing. The digest and the pruning of old records run every tick even while backed off.

## Quiet applications

`quietDays` in `config.yml` (default 14) is how many days an application may wait on the employer, after a step, before the UI points it out. The wait counts from when you marked the step done, from the booked time once it passed, or from when the last step closed. It counts only an in-process application whose latest step is done, a time that passed, or closed; an application with no step at all (just submitted, "No reply yet"), one with a step still to do (your move), one with an upcoming booked time, and one whose steps are only expired or cancelled are never quiet. It is a reminder only: Applications has a "Gone quiet" segment, a warning count in the sidebar and a notice, the job's lead suggests a nudge and Overview lists them. The app sends no follow-up, runs no agent for it, and the daily digest does not mention it.

## Daily question digest

`questionDigest` in `config.yml` defaults to enabled at **09:00 America/Chicago** (including daylight-saving changes). The scheduler sends new or changed question groups to the verified configured/connected Gmail mailbox. It does not send unchanged unanswered groups again, including when another application joins an already-notified group. The same message lists employers' steps still to do whose deadline falls within 48 hours (or has passed), each once per deadline, with a link to the application. There is no email when nothing new needs attention.

The message links to Questions and affected applications. Stored answers, question context values, credentials and sensitive non-question handoff summaries are excluded. **Settings → Send digest now** bypasses the schedule, not deduplication or the enabled setting. Settings also shows delivery errors. Each message carries a persisted delivery-reference footer and matching `X-Auto-Apply-Delivery-ID` header: Gmail can rewrite the RFC Message-ID. An uncertain delivery is reconciled against that exact identity, Sent label and verified self From/To before any retry; a missing confirmation does not trigger a blind resend until an hour has passed without a match, when the delivery becomes `failed`.

A delivery moves through `sending`, `uncertain`, `failed`, `sent` and `abandoned`, and only one is unfinished at a time. A `failed` delivery is sent again after 5 minutes, then 10, 20 and so on up to 6 hours between attempts (`attempts` counts them); after 5 failed attempts it is `abandoned` and the next digest starts fresh from whatever is still unnotified.

## Observation journal

[`reports/auto-apply-journal.md`](../reports/auto-apply-journal.md) records real auto-approved fill attempts, question stalls, cumulative themes, and suggestions for human review. `scripts/journal-stalls.ts` reads SQLite without changing applications or the knowledge base. Discovery, prep-only activity, and manually approved attempts are excluded; practice applications are real and included. Question gates indicate potentially missing or ambiguous guidance, not proof that an answer is absent. Theme classification uses text rules, not semantic knowledge-base validation.

Both stall and resume journals include direct auto approvals and approvals deferred by the daily cap and later reconsidered. Only jobs with attributable fill activity enter scope—not every queued approval. Existing stall checkpoints can discover previously omitted historical attempts on the next run.

The independent systemd user timer `auto-apply-journal.timer` checks every three hours at 01:00, 04:00, 07:00, 10:00, 13:00, 16:00, 19:00, and 22:00 local time. Persistent scheduling catches up after downtime. Checks without new eligible activity or changed resume-review evidence leave the journal unchanged. The initial entry covers retained eligible history; `data/auto-apply-journal-state.json` checkpoints subsequent stall observations rather than limiting them to a three-hour lookback.

Inspect the next check with `systemctl --user list-timers auto-apply-journal.timer`; run a check with `systemctl --user start auto-apply-journal.service`; stop monitoring with `systemctl --user disable --now auto-apply-journal.timer`. These controls do not restart or stop the application service. The journal includes execution-log commands. For isolated runs, use `bun scripts/journal-stalls.ts --db <path> --journal <path> --state <path>`.

The same service runs `scripts/journal-resumes.ts` afterward via `ExecStartPost`. It asks the model named in `scripts/resume-review.yml` to compare each actual auto-application's SHA-256-verified frozen PDF (extracted with `pdftotext`) against its stored posting and `profile/resume-notes.md`. It never substitutes the mutable stock resume. Missing or unattributable evidence is reported rather than guessed.

Resume findings separate supported omissions/underemphasized experience, unconfirmed experience worth asking about, and explicitly denied qualifications that must not be claimed. Cumulative rankings count distinct applications and companies, with required/core/preferred importance; quotes are resolved from source excerpt IDs. Classification and suggestions remain model judgments. This practice-biased sample is not a market-wide assessment, and posting text is the current saved description, not an independently frozen submission-time capture.

The reviewer has no tools, uses medium reasoning with no model fallback, and changes neither resumes nor application state. `prompts/resume-review.md` defines its review rules; `scripts/resume-review.yml` supplies its isolated runtime settings. Successful reviews are cached in `data/auto-apply-resume-reviews.json` by posting/resume/notes fingerprint. Unchanged inputs cause no model call or journal churn. Reviews run in batches of five, up to 25 applications per check; remaining work stays queued for the next check. Each model call is bounded to four minutes, and the combined service to 30 minutes. Failed reviews remain uncompleted and surface in the service log without discarding prior successful batches.

Run only the resume review with `bun scripts/journal-resumes.ts`. For isolated evidence, use `--db`, `--journal`, `--state`, `--notes`, and `--jobs-root`; see `--help` for batch controls. Running the systemd service executes both observers.

## Workday recovery

Long waits for answers can outlast a Workday login session. After an interruption or “Something went wrong”, sign in and let Candidate Home finish loading, then match the requisition and choose **Related Actions → Continue Application** on its **Not Submitted** draft. Wait for saved fields to populate and verify them before continuing; do not delete the draft or repeatedly restart Apply Manually/Autofill with Resume. A draft does not count as a submitted application, and the final approval gate still applies.

A frozen resume and local checkpoint do **not** prove that an employer account or draft exists. Maintenance before registration must not trap recovery in “missing original account.” `get_account` looks up the exact employer tenant; `{ register: true }` can create initial stored credentials only outside submission ambiguity. Workers distinguish documented pre-registration failures from existing applications, reuse saved credentials even if signup was interrupted, and stop registering if the employer reports an existing account. Workday credentials are employer-specific, not a global Workday login. Missing local credentials are not proof of no remote account.

The reproduced PwC failure returned HTTP 400 when restarting application creation, followed by HTTP 404 requests with an `undefined` application ID. Resuming the existing draft restored the form and reached a verified **Application Submitted / Application Received** state. This was an application/session recovery failure, not evidence of an anti-bot denial. The recovery instructions are in `prompts/fill.md`; new fill workers read that file at startup, while already-running workers retain their original prompt.
